# Japan Market Entry Proposal — 11-Page Structure

Concrete worked example of a first-meeting pitch for a Chinese hardware brand entering Japan via Makuake. This is a market entry argument, not a full campaign plan.

## When to Use This Pattern

Use when:
- Brand is skeptical about a new market ("our US KS did poorly, why would Japan be different?")
- Brand wants to benchmark against a specific competitor they name
- The meeting is 30 minutes with the Big Boss present
- The goal is to convince on strategy, not show execution detail

## 11-Page Structure

| Page | Title | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Cover | Establish presence. Product visual only, no company intro. |
| P2 | Core Judgment | **Directly answer the client's #1 doubt**: why a new market when the current one underperformed. Show KS→Makuake flip examples (Morus, Homerunpet, LISSOME, Sandbox, Oladance). US vs Japan narrative comparison table. |
| P3 | Product Essence | Prove you understand the product deeply — not feature recitation, but essence judgment. Three-column: phone-as-brain, desktop AI presence, 165W charging hub. Bottom line: "Trojan horse — daily utility delivering AI experience." |
| P4 | Current Narrative Problems | Light touch: analyze why the US narrative needs adjustment for Japan. NOT "diagnosing failure." Three points: AI co-worker too abstract for mass market, screen-aware education cost too high, 165W charging station not front-loaded enough. |
| P5 | Market Opportunity | Three data points proving Japan is structurally different: iOS share (59.65%), WFH rate (44.3%+), Makuake platform context (discovery vs innovation). Data sources cited for each. |
| P6 | Competitor Benchmark | If client names a competitor (e.g., Looi), do a structured comparison table. If your agency ran that competitor's campaign, LEAD with that. "We know this because we ran it." Show how the competitor validated the category, and how your client should own a different slice of it. |
| P7 | Japan Positioning Concept | The core Japanese concept in LARGE Japanese text. One sentence + one sub-sentence. Chinese explanation below. Dark background section for visual impact. |
| P8 | Three User Segments | Not abstract "Japanese consumers" — three concrete entry personas with JP tags, who they are, why they buy. |
| P9 | Makuake Page Narrative | 5-step narrative flow for the product page. Order matters: scene → product intro → AI reveal → function landing → purchase reason. |
| P10 | Launch Roadmap | 5-phase timeline (not Gantt, visual SVG timeline). Pre-launch → KOL/PR → Launch Day → Growth → Scale/Pivot. Bottom promise: "Every decision based on data." |
| P11 | Risks & Next Steps | Left: 4 known risks with mitigation. Right: 4 concrete next steps (NDA → deep brief → concept test → full proposal in 2 weeks). Final CTA in Japanese + Chinese. |

## Key Meeting Dynamics

- **P2 is the most critical page.** If the client's #1 concern is "our US KS didn't perform, why would Japan?", this page must answer it within 90 seconds. Lead with market structure difference, not product strength.
- **P6 competitor benchmark is the second most critical.** If your agency ran the competitor's campaign, this is your strongest trust signal. Put it in the first paragraph of P6 — don't bury it.
- **P7 Japanese concept is the anchor.** Everything before leads to it; everything after flows from it. Make it visually distinct (dark section).
- **Never criticize the client's original strategy.** P4 says "what we'd adjust for Japan," not "what you did wrong." Frame it as market difference, not market failure.

## Two-Step Workflow for First-Meeting Proposals

When the user provides a detailed plan/outline:

1. **Text content first** — write out every page's complete Chinese copy for review. The user will check tone, accuracy, and messaging before any design work happens. This avoids wasted HTML effort on content that needs revision.

2. **HTML after approval** — only after the user confirms the content, build the single-file HTML with all design elements (SVG charts, animations, sticky nav).

Do NOT skip to HTML before the user has seen and approved the text content.

## Language Rule

The plan doc may say "全程英文为主" — this refers to slide labels, section headers, and concept language (Japanese concepts stay in original). The body copy of the actual deliverable should follow the skill's standard: **Chinese primary**. When the user provides a plan that mixes English and Chinese guidance, default to Chinese for all body text unless the plan explicitly specifies English for a particular element (e.g., Japanese concept lines, data labels).
